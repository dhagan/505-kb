Based on the article comparing soft shackle methods, here is a summary of the 5 options (including the control) and how they rank when taking time and overall performance into account:

### Summary of Soft Shackle Methods

* **Spliced Loop (Control):**
* **Performance:** Achieved an average of $102.41\%$ of single line strength.
* **Time & Complexity:** Serves as a baseline test, but isn't a complete shackle on its own.


* **Method 1 - Diamond Knot:**
* **Performance:** Lowest strength of the shackle methods, averaging $139.63\%$ of single line strength.
* **Time & Complexity:** **The easiest and fastest method to tie**, requiring the least amount of rope and demonstrating the most consistent strength variance. However, tails are left on top, and the noose can be hard to open after being loaded.


* **Method 2 - The Better Diamond Knot:**
* **Performance:** Averages $147.78\%$ of single line strength.
* **Time & Complexity:** Uses the same quick Diamond Knot but modifies the noose structure to make it much easier to open and close. Like Method 1, it is quick to make compared to the high-strength options.


* **Method 3 - The Button Knot:**
* **Performance:** Exceptionally strong, averaging $224.44\%$ of single line strength.
* **Time & Complexity:** **Takes a long time to make** (primarily due to the time-consuming process of tapering and burying the tails). However, it is easy to tie once you learn the knot, leaves no tails on top, and provides a large diameter body with great ease of opening. This is the method the author recommends and uses professionally.


* **Method 4 - The Big Overhand:**
* **Performance:** Highest average strength at $228.24\%$ of single line strength (though it has high variance and can be delicate if not tied perfectly).
* **Time & Complexity:** **Takes the longest to make**—averaging around 20 minutes per shackle—because it requires creating two separate eye splices.



---

### Which is Best?

* **If time is your primary constraint:** **Method 1 (Diamond Knot)** or **Method 2 (Better Diamond Knot)** are the clear winners because they require significantly less rope, fewer steps, and take a fraction of the time to tie. Method 2 gives you the added benefit of an easier-to-open noose without adding massive build time.
* **If you want the best balance of strength and practical use (and don't mind spending extra time):** **Method 3 (The Button Knot)** is considered the best overall choice by the author. While it takes longer to construct due to the tailed buries, it delivers massive strength, a smooth knot structure with no annoying top tails, and easy handling after heavy loads.
